# Decision policy

Recommendations are generated by `src/features/recommendations/engine.ts` from a US AQI value and household choices. Each action has a stable ID, category, priority, reason codes and evidence IDs. The interface can explain why it was included. Sources are maintained in `src/features/recommendations/evidence.ts`; AI-generated references are checked against the actions supplied to the model.

For missing, invalid or stale air data, the app asks the user to check an official source and continues to offer independent indoor smoke guidance. Above US AQI 100, the policy adds caution for sensitive groups; above 150, or above 100 for a household marked sensitive or with children, it recommends reducing prolonged or strenuous outdoor activity. Above 200 it also adds an essential-trips precaution. Commute and cooking choices can add contextual actions. These are general decision rules, not individualized medical advice.

## Planning an outing

The planner compares complete hourly forecast windows inside the user's selected period, for durations of one to four hours. It selects the window with the lowest peak AQI, breaking ties by the earlier start. It excludes missing or duplicate hourly readings. For the purpose of this comparison, windows above 100 for sensitive households or above 150 for other households are marked unsuitable for flexible activities; essential trips can still show the lowest available window with an elevated-risk warning. A lower forecast is not a guarantee of safety.

The daily timeline provides broad routine suggestions and is not a full optimizer of every obligation. Indoor guidance is based on household answers and available outdoor conditions; it does not infer a measured indoor AQI. Sources include [AirNow](https://www.airnow.gov/aqi/aqi-basics/), [EPA indoor air guidance](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq) and [EPA near-road air pollution information](https://www.epa.gov/air-research/near-roadway-air-pollution-and-health-frequently-asked-questions).

The policy is conservative product guidance, not clinically reviewed or a substitute for a clinician's advice. Decisions and thresholds should be independently reviewed before use in health-critical settings.
